# Aaspaas: Learn Course, Daily Clues, Practice Portfolio & AI Thinking Partner

Aaspaas becomes a learning loop: free video lessons (on stocks, tax and investment products), daily "what people are noticing" clues, and an AI thinking partner, all open to everyone. Practice portfolios run on pretend money and need a verified email: up to 3 free, with more coming in Aaspaas Plus.
Every feature stays on the education side of SEBI rules. Readers make their own choices, and Aaspaas never ranks picks, says buy or sell, or builds a portfolio for anyone.

## Who it's for
- **New graduates and first-time earners** who don't yet understand margins, ROCE, tax on shares, or the difference between a SIP and an FD.
- **Visitors from YouTube, Instagram and Google** who arrive on a lesson or a daily clue and stay to try the idea on real companies.
- **Returning readers** who check today's clues, how their practice portfolio is doing against the Nifty 50, and whether their reasoning holds up.
- **The founder**, who needs Studio to show which content brings people in and what keeps them coming back.

## What changed from the earlier ideas (and why)
| Idea | What Aaspaas does instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rank stocks / say which of 5 brands "has more potential" | The reader sorts by one sourced number they choose. A side-by-side view of similar brands says where each is strongest and weakest, with no overall pick. (Phase 2) | Naming picks or "potential" is a research recommendation and needs SEBI Research Analyst registration. |
| Ask for a commitment to invest, then build a portfolio | A **practice portfolio**: ₹1,00,000 of pretend cash that the reader splits across companies they choose, with a written "why I'm adding this" note. | Building someone a portfolio is investment advice. Practising with pretend money teaches the same discipline. |
| An AI "CA" | An AI **thinking partner** that questions the reader's reasoning and flags bias, but never says buy, sell or hold. | "CA" is a protected professional title, and advice requires registration. |
| Ask for email before a practice portfolio, max 3 before Plus | Done as asked. The email is **verified** with a sign-in email, which also opens the reader's portfolios, discoveries and notes on any device. A 4th portfolio leads to the Plus waitlist. | An unverified email would let anyone open someone else's portfolio by typing their address, and would fill the list with fake addresses. |
| Course on Instagram instead of YouTube | Full lessons stay on **YouTube**. Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts work as short teasers that link back to each lesson page. | YouTube videos show up in search, can be embedded properly and suit longer lessons. Reels are better for reach than for teaching. |

## Core features and experience

### 1. Learn hub and lesson pages (`/learn`)
- **Three modules, set out as chapters in a course book**
  - **Module 1 · How a business makes money (Sharma Chai's first year)**
    1. Revenue vs profit
    2. Margins: what's left from each ₹100 of sales
    3. Cash flow: why profit isn't the same as cash in the bank
    4. The balance sheet in 60 seconds
  - **Module 2 · Is it a good business?**
    5. ROCE ✅
    6. ROE, and how debt can flatter it
    7. Debt: good loans vs dangerous ones
    8. Moat: why customers keep coming back
    9. Management: how they spend the profits
    10. Quality of growth: sales growth vs profit growth
  - **Module 3 · Money basics for new earners** (new)
    11. Your first demat and trading account: what a broker actually does
    12. Stocks, mutual funds, index funds and ETFs: what you really own
    13. SIPs: investing a fixed amount every month
    14. FD, PPF, EPF and NPS: the steady side of money
    15. Tax on shares: short-term gains taxed at 20%, long-term gains at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year
    16. Tax on dividends, mutual funds and debt funds
    17. Old vs new tax regime, and where ELSS fits
    18. F&O and intraday: why about 9 in 10 individual traders lose money (SEBI's own study)
- **Lesson cards** show the number, title, video length, a one-line promise and a "watched" tick. Ticks are saved in this browser, with no login.
- **Lesson pages** have their own web address (e.g. `/learn/roce`, `/learn/tax-on-shares`). Each one has:
  - **Video:** a YouTube thumbnail that loads the player only on tap.
  - **Written notes:** 2–3 short paragraphs in the Sharma Chai story, one ₹ worked example, and a "check it yourself" list. This text is what Google ranks.
  - **"See it on real companies":** the lesson's metric shown live for today's top trending clue's companies and their rivals, using sourced Yahoo data. Lessons 1–10 only.
  - **Next steps:** "Next lesson", "Try it on your own clue", "Add a company to your practice portfolio", and a share button (WhatsApp, X, copy link).
  - **"Also on Instagram"** link, when a Reel exists.
  - **Tax lessons (15–17)** also carry "General information, not tax advice · rates as of FY 2026-27 · last checked [date]".
- **Lessons without a video yet** show "Video coming soon" and still have their full written notes.
- **Optional "Email me new lessons" box:** uses the same verified email sign-in with the updates box ticked. Consent is recorded; no newsletter is sent yet.
- **Navigation:** a "Learn" link in every header.
- **Search and link previews:** every lesson and every daily clue page has its own title, description and preview image, and all are listed in a sitemap.

### 2. Lessons linked where they're needed
- **Peer table:** each metric explainer gets a "Watch the lesson" link:
  - Net profit → lesson 1
  - Op. margin → lesson 2
  - Op. cash flow → lesson 3
  - ROCE → lesson 5
  - ROE → lesson 6
  - Debt/equity → lesson 7
  - Rev. growth → lesson 10
- **Financials tab:** the cash-vs-profit sentence links to lesson 3, and the year tables link to lesson 4.
- **Business lenses:**
  - Moat → lesson 8
  - Management and Capital allocation → lesson 9
  - Growth runway → lesson 10
- **Practice portfolio:**
  - The portfolio page links to lesson 13 (SIPs) and lesson 15 (tax on shares).
  - Each "sell" shows a plain note on whether the gain would be short- or long-term if it were real money, with a link to lesson 15.
- **Waiting screen:** one rotating card says "While you wait: watch lesson X". It opens in a new tab.

### 3. Launch videos
- "Watch how Aaspaas works" button on the landing page, next to the observation box, and on the friend landing page. It opens the video in a dark overlay.
- Studio has a slot for each placement. Changing the video means pasting a new YouTube link.

### 4. Daily "What people are noticing" clues
- **What it reads:** every morning (India time), Aaspaas reads Google Trends India's public "trending now" feed (up to 10 items) and 2–3 free Indian business-news feeds.
- **What it picks:** the AI chooses up to **5** things an ordinary person could notice (products, launches, habits, shop prices, festival or weather effects) and rewrites each as an everyday observation.
- **What it always skips:**
  - politics, crime, deaths and disasters
  - sports results and celebrity gossip
  - anything that is itself about stock prices, tips, top gainers, IPO hype or results-day moves
- **Ready to open:** each clue is run through the normal discovery ahead of time (companies, rivals, sourced data). It opens instantly and does not use the visitor's 3-a-day allowance.
- **Clue cards** show:
  - the observation
  - "Spotted in: Google Trends India" or the headline source, with a link
  - the date and the tickers
  - "Open the read" and "Use as my own clue"
- **Where clues appear:** a "Today people are noticing" strip on the landing page, and a `/today` page with a dated archive. Each day has its own shareable page.
- **If the feeds fail,** the previous day's clues stay up, clearly dated.

### 5. Sign in with email (needed only for practice portfolios)
- **Where it's asked:** when a signed-out reader taps "Start a practice portfolio" or "Add to practice portfolio", a short sheet asks for their email. Its heading is "Save your practice portfolios and open them on any device".
- **Updates consent:** a separate, unticked checkbox reads "Also email me new lessons and Aaspaas updates". Saying no still gives full access.
- **The sign-in email**, sent from "Aaspaas", contains:
  - a **one-tap sign-in link** (valid 15 minutes, works once)
  - a **6-digit code** for the same sign-in. Many phone mail apps open links in their own browser, so the reader can instead type the code in the tab where they started.
- **After signing in:**
  - The reader comes back exactly where they were, and the company they were adding is ready to confirm.
  - Everything this browser already had (discoveries, field notes, thinking-partner chats, watched lessons) joins their account.
- **Any device:** signing in on another phone or laptop shows the same portfolios, discoveries and notes. A device stays signed in for 90 days unless the reader signs out.
- **Header:** a small account menu shows the reader's email, "My practice portfolios", "My Discoveries" and "Sign out". Signed-out readers see a quiet "Sign in" link.
- **Abuse protection:** at most 3 sign-in emails per address per hour and 10 per network per hour. After 5 wrong codes, the code expires.
- **Emails are sign-in only:** nothing else is sent in this release, and Aaspaas never asks anyone to send a password or code back.
- **Everything else stays open without an email:** lessons, daily clues, discoveries and the thinking partner.

### 6. Practice portfolios (pretend money, up to 3 free)
- **Getting started:** a signed-in reader can create up to **3 practice portfolios**, each with a name they choose (e.g. "Kirana staples", "EV hunch") and **₹1,00,000 of pretend cash**. Each is labelled everywhere as "Practice · pretend money · no real trades".
- **Switching:** the portfolio page has a switcher between the reader's portfolios. When adding a company, the reader picks which portfolio it goes into.
- **The 4th portfolio:** trying to create one shows "More portfolios come with Aaspaas Plus (coming soon)" with a one-tap "Join the Plus waitlist". Joining records interest; nothing is charged. Deleting a portfolio frees its slot.
- **Adding a company:** use "Add to practice portfolio" on any price card, peer-table row (rivals included) or company detail page. The reader enters a ₹ amount.
  - **Buying:** the company is "bought" at the latest available adjusted close. Fractional units are allowed, so the ₹ amount is exact.
  - **"Why I'm adding this":** a one-line note is required. It is the reader's own reason, kept with the holding.
  - **"What would change my mind":** an optional line, plus an optional "check back on" date.
- **The portfolio page** (`/portfolio`) shows:
  - total value, cash left, and total gain/loss in ₹ and %
  - a line chart of the portfolio against the **Nifty 50**, as if the same rupees had gone into the index on the same days
  - each holding's current value, gain/loss and holding period, with the reader's note shown under it
  - a factual sector mix, e.g. "62% of your practice money is in one sector", with no advice on what to change
- **Reader actions:** "Sell part / all" (cash returns to the balance), "Reset portfolio", "Rename", "Delete", and "Ask the thinking partner about this holding".
- **"Check back" reminders** appear inside Aaspaas when the reader returns. No emails or notifications.
- **No real money, ever:** no broker link, no real orders, no leaderboard of readers' returns, and no "suggested" holdings or amounts.
- **Storage:** saved to the reader's account and available on any signed-in device.

### 7. AI thinking partner
- **Where to find it:** "Challenge my thinking" on company detail pages, on each practice-portfolio holding, and on the recap.
- **How it starts:** the reader writes their view, e.g. "Britannia will keep growing because every kirana stocks it". The partner replies in four labelled parts:
  1. **Supported by the numbers:** cites the sourced figures Aaspaas shows (peer table, Financials, price history), each labelled as Yahoo data.
  2. **Still an assumption:** what the reader is taking on trust.
  3. **Possible bias:** names it plainly, e.g. familiarity bias ("you see it everywhere"), recency, anchoring on the share price, confirmation, or fear of missing out.
  4. **Questions to answer next:** 2–3 questions, plus what evidence would change the view, linked to the matching lesson where one fits.
- **Follow-ups:** the reader can reply. A conversation runs up to 6 back-and-forth turns.
- **Hard limits:**
  - It never says buy, sell or hold, never gives price targets, position sizes or predictions, and never calls itself a CA or adviser.
  - Asked "should I buy?", it explains it can't advise and turns the question back to the reader's own checklist.
  - It uses only Aaspaas's own sourced data and the company context. It never invents numbers, and it says when it doesn't know.
- **Labelling:** every reply is in the indigo "AI context" style, with "Educational reasoning help, not financial advice".
- **Daily allowance:** 10 messages per visitor per day, plus a site-wide daily cap. Reaching the limit shows a calm "come back tomorrow" note.
- **Saving:** conversations are saved to My Discoveries in this browser. When the reader is signed in, they're saved to their account and show on any device.

### 8. Instagram-ready content
- Studio can download a **portrait 1080×1350 image** for each daily clue (observation, tickers, date) and for each lesson (title, key idea, Aaspaas link). The founder posts these, and Reels, manually.
- Each lesson in Studio stores an optional Instagram Reel link, which shows as "Also on Instagram" on the lesson page.
- Links from Instagram bios and stories carry a source tag, so Studio can show Instagram traffic separately.

### 9. Studio additions
- **Lessons manager:**
  - per lesson: YouTube link, Reel link, length, written notes (pre-drafted, editable), published on/off, and a "last checked" date for tax lessons
  - add new lessons and modules
- **Launch-video slots.**
- **Trending log:** raw feed items, which were picked or skipped and why, a "hide this clue" switch, and Instagram image downloads.
- **New metrics:**
  - lesson views by source (Google, YouTube, Instagram, WhatsApp, direct)
  - video plays and completion
  - lesson → own clue
  - trending clue opens and → own clue
  - email sign-in funnel: sheet shown → email entered → sign-in completed (link vs code), and the share who also opted into updates
  - signed-in readers, readers using 2+ devices, and email send failures
  - practice portfolios per reader (1 / 2 / 3), how often the 3-portfolio limit is hit, and Plus waitlist joins from that limit
  - practice portfolios started, average holdings, return visits, sells, resets
  - thinking-partner conversations and messages, and the share of "should I buy?" questions that were redirected
  - daily AI cost for trending clues and the thinking partner
  - lesson email sign-ups

## User flow
1. **From YouTube or Instagram:** a viewer watches the ROCE Reel, taps the bio link and lands on `/learn/roce` (counted as Instagram traffic). They read the notes, see ROCE for today's clue companies and their rivals, then tap "Try it on your own clue".
2. **Daily return:** on the landing page, "Today people are noticing" shows five dated clues. The reader opens one and the full read appears instantly.
   - They tap "Add to practice portfolio" on Britannia. The email sheet asks for their address, and they tick "also email me new lessons".
   - They tap the link in their inbox (or type the 6-digit code) and land back on Britannia.
   - They create "Kirana staples" and add Britannia with ₹20,000 and the note "every kirana stocks it".
   - Later they sign in on their laptop and see the same portfolio.
3. **Thinking it through:** they tap "Challenge my thinking". The partner shows which sourced numbers support the view, flags familiarity bias, and asks how Britannia's margins compare with Nestle's and ITC's in the peer table. It links lesson 2.
4. **Weeks later:** they have made "EV hunch" and "Festive season" too. Trying a 4th shows the Plus waitlist, which they join in one tap. The portfolio page shows each practice portfolio against the Nifty 50. A "check back" reminder sits next to Britannia. Selling shows a "this would be a short-term gain if real" note linking lesson 15.
5. **New grad path:** a Google search for "LTCG tax on shares" lands on lesson 15. The reader works through Module 3 and signs up for new-lesson emails.

## UI/UX feel
- **Learn:** a calm, paper-toned course book. Large serif lesson numbers, a thin marigold progress line per module, the Sharma Chai thread, and a small chai-stall motif. Module 3 uses a ledger/passbook motif for money basics.
- **Lesson pages:** read like a short article. The video comes first; then a monospace "KEY IDEA" label and the notes. The live real-company panel uses the marigold sourced-data style.
- **Trending strip:** dated "field note" clippings with a monospace date stamp, source tag, serif observation and ticker chips. It can be swiped sideways on phones.
- **Practice portfolio:** a quiet ledger look, with clear "PRACTICE · PRETEND MONEY" stamping. Gain and loss colours are used only for real positive and negative numbers. The Nifty 50 appears as a dashed neutral line.
- **Email sheet:** a short bottom sheet on phones and a small centred card on desktop. It has one email field, the unticked updates checkbox, and a plain line: "We only use this to sign you in, and to send updates if you tick the box". It is followed by a calm "Check your inbox" screen with the 6-digit code boxes.
- **Thinking partner:** a calm chat panel in the indigo AI register. Each reply shows its four labelled parts as small sections rather than a wall of text. The reader's notes appear in ink, and cited figures appear in the marigold data style.
- **Video overlay:** dark and cinematic, like the story deck.
- **Everything is built for phones first,** with light and dark themes both checked.

## Assumptions
- **Education-only stance (agreed):** no rankings of picks, no "best of five", no portfolios built for readers, no buy/sell/hold, and the AI never calls itself a CA.
- **Partnerships:** before any broker or affiliate partnership, a legal check is needed. SEBI restricts regulated firms from working with education platforms that show recent share-price data.
- **Video:** full lessons on YouTube; Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts as teasers. Aaspaas only embeds; it never hosts video files.
- **Lesson notes:** drafted by us for all 18 lessons, editable by the founder. Only lesson 5 (ROCE) has a video at launch.
- **Tax lessons:** use FY 2026-27 rules. Budget 2026 made no changes: listed shares and equity funds are taxed at 20% on short-term gains (12 months or less) and 12.5% on long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year, plus 4% cess. They are always labelled general information with a "last checked" date.
- **Daily clues:** at most 5 per day, made automatically each morning with no approval step but with a hide switch. They use their own small daily AI budget, separate from visitor discoveries. Sources are free public feeds; Google's official Trends API is invite-only and not used.
- **Practice portfolios:**
  - Prices are the latest available adjusted close (delayed), and tracking uses adjusted close, so dividends are reflected in value.
  - Up to 15 holdings per portfolio, with Indian-listed companies only for now.
  - Up to 3 portfolios per verified email. Deleting one frees a slot; resetting does not use one up.
- **Email sign-in:**
  - Uses Emergent's built-in email sending, so no setup or keys are needed. The sender name is "Aaspaas" on a platform-managed address.
  - Sign-in links last 15 minutes and work once. Devices stay signed in for 90 days.
  - Update emails are opt-in only, through a separate unticked box, in line with India's data-protection consent rules.
  - Studio shows counts, never a list of individual emails, until the Phase 2 export.
- **Thinking partner:** runs on Gemini 2.5 Flash, already connected, so no new keys are needed. It has 10 messages per visitor per day and a site-wide daily cap, and costs show in Studio.
- **Sign-in is optional except for portfolios:** without signing in, watched ticks, discoveries and conversations stay with this browser, as today. Signing in joins them to the account.
- **Email:** the "Email me new lessons" box on the Learn pages uses the same verified sign-in. Ticking updates there records consent. No newsletter is sent in Phase 1.
